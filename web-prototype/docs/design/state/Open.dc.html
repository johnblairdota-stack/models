The one that decides whether there is a game
No player holds private information that anyone else wants.
Over a full five-episode eight-player game: 126 log entries, 116 of them public, and every player receives exactly one private event in the whole game — their own role card. Across 31 phase-frames × 8 phones the only non-you difference anywhere is the guide hearing their own spoken word echoed back. So the Debrief is eight people reasoning about six facts they all share.

The mode bible asks that every event have two plausible explanations. The build passes that and fails the precondition it never states: there has to be more than one event.

TERMINAL_AT is six anchor names and TERMINAL_REACH is a 2.2 m disc, so the objective exists only as a coordinate. Blind-sweep worst case measured at 21 s at RUN in the ballroom. Meanwhile lit — the camera unlock the entire win condition runs on — is delivered with no on-screen cause.

safe-hold
The phone sends an absolute compass bearing, unthrottled
The UX spec calls for yaw rate at 20 Hz capped at MOVE.turnRate. An absolute bearing has no idle value, no rate cap and no "thumb is off the stick" signal — which is why the drivable-frame contract had to key off throttle alone. With no ack or retransmit, a dropped packet latches the last bearing and drives the robot into a wall. One job, not two.

Accessibility, measured

The eight-seat palette is not colour-blind safe
Viénot/Brettel with CIEDE2000 across all 28 pairs: seat 2 vs seat 5 is ΔE 3.3 under deuteranopia — the same colour. Amber and teal are 0.7 L* apart, so luminance does not rescue it. Roughly a one-in-three chance per evening with four men on a sofa. The gate is worth more than the repalette. _room-probe3.mjs
At 8 m the evidence board is at the acuity limit
On a 55" 1080p panel: public claim 6.5 arcmin, RUNNER/GUIDE badge 5.5, footer 4.6 — against 5 for detection and ~16 for comfortable reading. And the seat number inside the dot, the non-colour channel that saves the deuteranope, is 5.5. The channel that rescues one failure fails first at distance.
