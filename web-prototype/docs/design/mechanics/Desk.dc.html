The lever
One gain toggle — NEAR hears everything in the runner's room and one door out, WIDE hears the whole house but only gunshots. And one spoken verb that is worth more than the toggle: “stop moving, I can’t hear.” The runner's own throttle sets the guide's noise floor, so asking for silence is a real, public, costly request against a 90-second clock.
How an honest guide is wrong
Three ways, all physical. A Hunter that has noticed you stops dead and makes no sound — so the quiet moments are the dangerous ones. The runner's own noise masks: at RUN nothing under a half-second-old breach survives. And outside camera coverage a sound is a band across two rooms with no name on it.
How the table argues
This is the one that fixes the argument rather than the screen. The crash is already public — the TV prints LOUD CRASH — THE LONG GALLERY and the audio never cuts. So the table can finally say “you had the same crash I did.” And the best defence in the game comes from someone else's public behaviour: “she was at RUN, of course he couldn't hear.”
What it costs
simReport() already carries runner.noise and the Hunter's position every tick, so nothing under src/game/ is touched. About forty lines inside sightForGuide(), a canvas that draws rings, one constant.
Where it breaks
The floor is made of the Hunter's stationary duty cycle, and nobody has measured it. If it reads under 0.10, full coverage is an oracle again and the whole case collapses. It also narrows what makes the guide unique: the room now hears a coarse version of what they see.
