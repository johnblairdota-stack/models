## Phase 2 — the dig vertical slice (`docs/design/dig.md`)

John's newest and largest direction, and it **structurally fixes a defect a critic proved by
playing**: today you find the exit by *tapping each door for one second* — breachable falls over,
chained never flinches, exit chews but holds. **The search costs less than the payoff.** Under dig,
most digs bottom out at a barrier, so an expensive dud finally exists.

Build in this order:
1. **Segmented walls** (Phase 1's output) + the **depth falloff** — big chunks shallow, chips deep.
   ⚠️ **`STAGE_DEFS` is already this gradient** (wallpaper → plaster → lath → beam, healths
   40/70/55/**90**) — tune it, do not invent it.
2. **The brick barrier**: ordinary segments bottom out at `barrier`, only the interconnect reaches
   `open`. **Two-sided — model it once per shared EDGE**, never twice. ⚠️ Two barriers kept in
   agreement is a desync waiting to happen and the ids are a network protocol surface.
   ✅ `refs/lath/lath-tower-of-london.jpg` and `demo-selective-12.jpg` already photograph
   lath-and-plaster in front of masonry — **the brick is the barrier, and it is already the bar**.
3. **The interconnect** at the base of the wall (no vertical axis exists — a low gap is a passage on
   the floor plane) + **the house-wide unlock**.
4. **The hunter crosses the barrier** — it is a *robot* barrier. Mostly wiring `_tooNarrowPanel`,
   which already makes a stage-3 hunter breach rather than squeeze.
5. **Re-measure everything it invalidates**: `eo2-siege.mjs`, `pc7-play.mjs`. The honest metric
   becomes **time to FIND the interconnect**, including duds — not time to open one segment.
